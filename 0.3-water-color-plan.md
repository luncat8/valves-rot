# 0.3 water colors plan

## Goal and scope

Extend the water grid with red, green and blue liquid, optional separation by color, and two views. This is a Lab water feature, not a change to grain colors or hue quests. Keep the existing grid, mask, supply, displacement and drainage model; it is quasi-static, not a falling-water solver. Scripts remain classic, usable from `file://`, with guarded Node exports.

## State and transport

Liquid is conserved per channel. One `Float64Array ch[count * 3]` holds the R, G, B slice areas of each cell (stride 3, so a cell's three values sit together in the move loop), and `trappedCh` holds the same for volume that a closing wall is carrying. The cell total `vol[i]` stays as a cache of that sum, so the level solver, displacement and every existing reader of a total keep their shape; the channels are authoritative and `max |vol[i] - sum(ch[i])|` over all cells must stay at rounding level (a residual is published by the fixtures, not assumed).

Exactly one primitive moves liquid, `move(w, from, to, area)`: it splits `area` across the donor's channels in proportion, gives the last channel the remainder so the moved amount is exact, and updates both totals. There is no second way to move liquid, so proportional transfer cannot be forgotten on one path — vertical fill, horizontal sill spill, the closed-cell push, trapped return, supply, overflow and drain all bottom out in `move` or in `deposit(w, cell, area, shares)` (the only other way volume enters a cell). Never reconstruct color from a rendered pixel, and never assign a channel implicitly: fixtures and the vent state their shares explicitly.

Keep the existing counters and add a per-channel book beside each: `attempted`, `unadmitted` (supply), `admitted`, `overflowed`, `drained`, plus `initial` for volume a fixture or a scenario imposes directly. Melting and freezing later add conversion counters of their own. For each channel, `initial + admitted + melted = stored (including trapped) + overflowed + drained + frozen`, with the scalar totals for the panel taken as the sums of the books. A refused supply is `unadmitted`, never `admitted`; drain and reset cover trapped shares too.

Three pour-color checkboxes select equal shares of the inlet rate; default all selected, and the last one cannot be deselected. Shares are normalized at the module boundary (`setPour` keeps the previous mask when asked for none), so an empty selection never silently falls back to a default. The existing single-rate control still specifies total volume. Change existing direct-injection fixtures to assign shares through `setLiquid`/`inject` explicitly.

## Mixability and density

Three independent mixable checkboxes, initially checked. Checked channels form one homogeneous phase with effective density 1000 kg/m³; each unchecked channel is its own phase, with R 2000, G 1000, B 500 kg/m³ — the grain palette's own values (dense red, neutral, buoyant blue), so 0.4 melt and freeze move between materials of one density family and a blue grain hangs neutral in blue liquid. Green liquid at 1000 is the neutral *teal* grain's density, not the 800 kg/m³ green grain; the liquids are named by hue, the densities by family, and the Help says so.

Phases are derived, not stored. The mixability flags define a partition of the three channels (at most three groups, at most three phases in a cell), so a cell's phase volume is the sum of its channels in that group and the density order is a sort of at most three values. A checkbox change therefore cannot lose or move volume: nothing is rewritten, only the partition changes, and repeated toggles are bit-neutral by construction. Split or merge takes effect on the next step without altering channel inventories. Miscible channels are assumed to homogenize instantly, so unchecking and rechecking a channel returns the mixture it came from.

This is an illustrative effective-density model: channel volume is conserved on toggling, not a physical equation of state. Keeping the miscible phase at a fixed 1000 also makes the all-mixable solver identical to the single-liquid baseline, which is the regression gate for every later change; a volume-weighted blend density is a possible later knob, not a default.

Extend the vertical fill/lower-first relaxation to exchange phases in density order, including a heavier phase above a lighter phase in a filled column. An unstable neighbour pair swaps equal slice areas of its two phases across the face — heavy down, light up — capped by both availabilities and a fraction of cell capacity, so a light layer buried under a heavy one rises over a few ticks, quasi-statically and exactly per channel, never through a closed seam. A swap keeps each cell's total, so the published surface does not move with it. Vertical swaps alone are not enough: heavy beside light at one surface is the lock-exchange setup, the surface solver reads it as settled and freezes, and the light phase stays trapped beside the heavy one. Across horizontal faces the heavier cell therefore trades its heaviest phase for the lighter cell's lightest down the lateral mean-density gradient, in equal areas so both totals keep, capped so the means land at worst equal and never cross; the vertical swaps then cash the traded phases into sorted columns. Equal-density phases never trade, so a mixed pool stays still. Sill spills walk the source's phases top-down against one budget. Trapped liquid retains its composition and is sorted on reopening. Reuse preallocated per-phase scratch arrays; do not allocate in the step loop.

Publish per-phase vertical boundaries alongside the existing free surface. Apply grain buoyancy as `g * (1 - sum(rho_layer/rho_grain * submerged_fraction_in_layer))`, computing band fractions as differences of the existing circular-segment function. Preserve existing drag using total wetted fraction. Account for displacement in layer capacity with an overlap-weighted approximation consistent with the current grid: keep the cell total `gdisp` that decides the free surface, and charge each grain's submerged displaced area into the phase band that holds its submerged centroid, clamped so no phase capacity goes negative. Record that approximation and test displaced capacity for no negative inventory. When all colors are mixable, identical-density results must agree with the old single-liquid solver within numerical tolerance.

## Views

Use the existing mask/display sampling path, not per-texel geometry queries. Compute the normalized channel triple once per water cell per frame, `I[k] = 255 * ch[k] / max(ch[R], ch[G], ch[B])` (a byte triple in the display field, zero where the cell is empty), so the texel loop stays a byte copy plus the existing depth alpha. This dominant-normalized display preserves ratios while allowing R+G to be yellow and equal R+G+B to be white. It is a visualization, not a physical additive-light intensity or a measure of liquid volume.

Sample the phase at texel y, using its channel shares for color and retaining the existing surface and depth alpha; empty cells remain transparent. The display field stores up to three dense-first triples per cell with their band boundaries (in slice 1, one phase, so the cell triple is the phase triple), and each texel walks at most two boundaries to its band.

The color view paints that triple at a fixed 55% shade, because an equal mix is white and white over pale porcelain reads as a brighter wall rather than as liquid: the ratio and hue are the mapping, the shade is a display convention that keeps every mix darker than the ceramic (a pure channel stays its own hue). The field's triple itself stays unshaded, so the pigment view and the fixtures test the mapping and not the shade.

A Color/Pigment radio is display-only. Color displays `I`. Pigment displays neutral gray with `L = clamp((765 - (I.R + I.G + I.B)) / 2, 0, 255)` from the same byte triple, rounded to byte, as a pure function so the anchors are unit-testable: an RGB input of (80,80,80) clamps to 255, (255,255,0) maps to about 128 and (255,255,255) to 0. Note that normalized equal channel shares display as (255,255,255), not (80,80,80), and pure primaries map to light. A separated pure band is therefore light in Pigment mode. View changes must never affect state or dynamics, and the free-surface stroke stays as the level indicator in both views (it is the only part of the sheet that is not a channel color). Explain normalization and these consequences in Help.

## Slices and acceptance gates

1. **Channels and views.** Add the channel array, the move/deposit primitives, per-channel books, inlet choices and both views with all colors mixed. `node experiments/water-color-fixtures.cjs`: conservation per channel through all transfer, close/reopen, supply, overflow, drain, reset and trapped reopen; `vol` residual at rounding level; nonnegative finite values; proportional ratios preserved by every move; normalized-triple, shaded paint and pigment anchors above; supply split by the pour mask; toggling views leaves a channel snapshot unchanged. Existing water state, buoyancy and rendering smoke fixtures remain green after their injection helpers move to `setLiquid`. First half (state and books) is testable before any pixel changes; the view half follows once the state fixtures are green.
2. **Separated phases.** Add checkboxes, phase sorting, sill transport, layer display and buoyancy. `node experiments/water-color-phase.cjs`: an inverted R/G/B stack orders (tube 5 s, basin 10 s budgets, ≥2× the measured ~1 s / ~5 s) with heavy-over-light residue under 1e-3 px²; blue poured under red reaches the top half in 5 s; a 2:1 R+G mix keeps its ratio to 2e-16 while blue layers above; a shut gate passes zero px² over 5 s and holds bit-exact; split/merge and trapped reopen return every channel; one-step grain accelerations match the band-summed analytic expression to 6e-7 (teal −g in red, +g/2 in blue, blue 0 in blue); the interface cell ranks red/blue with texels splitting at the published band boundary; an emptied below-surface cell keeps finite bands from below. `node experiments/water-mixed-baseline.cjs` holds the all-mixable golden bit-identical (stored 800.0, checksum, surface) through every phase-code change.
3. **Scenarios and measurement.** Add `experiments/water-color-balance.cjs` and `experiments/water-color-picture.cjs`; record reproducible commands, seed, time step, hardware and numbers in `experiments/logs/`. Capture mixed and layered examples in both views, including a gate closure. Run existing water separation/gate/buoyancy and render smoke fixtures. Check repeated toggles and long-running closed-box channel residuals, as well as a wet-step and mask-rebuild timing comparison against the baseline.

## Balance and limits

Acceptance is measured, not assumed: publish max absolute and relative channel residuals and tolerances, closed-column ordering and settling time, mixed-only baseline deltas, and timing before/after. Baseline water observations include 0/24 floats entering a surface-level side pocket, gate capture of 4/4 floats and 6/6 dense grains, roughly 0.20 ms dry / 1.37 ms turning / 0.32 ms wet step and a 16 ms mask rebuild on the recorded machine; these are comparisons, not guarantees on other hardware. Slice 2 re-measured on its own scene and machine, before/after on identical runs: wet static 0.316 → 0.424 ms/step, wet turning 0.420 → 0.593 ms/step, mask rebuild 0.40 → 0.36 ms (unchanged within noise), display sample 1.042 → 1.129 ms; a settling tube costs 0.436 ms/step with three sorting phases against 0.320 all-mixable. The step cost is the band publish plus band-summed buoyancy the layers require, still a small fraction of the 8.33 ms step budget. The grid has no resolved droplets, turbulence, interfacial tension, molecular diffusion or calibrated mixing kinetics. Layering and displacement are approximations, especially at narrow seams and under ceilings. A phase is a group of channels, so a "layer" thinner than one grid cell exists only as a share of that cell, not as geometry.

The long-run balance measurement must also report the minimum channel value:
repeated partition changes can expose negative floating-point dust even when the
ledger closes. Strict nonnegative inventories remain a goal; do not mistake a
small residual tolerance for proof of strict nonnegativity. Capture gate closure
separately from the mixed/layered view comparison, and identify each panel's
state and view in the reproducible command or accompanying log.
