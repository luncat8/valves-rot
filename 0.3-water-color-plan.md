# 0.3 water colors plan

## Goal and scope

Extend the water grid with red, green and blue liquid, optional separation by color, and two views. This is a Lab water feature, not a change to grain colors or hue quests. Keep the existing grid, mask, supply, displacement and drainage model; it is quasi-static, not a falling-water solver. Scripts remain classic, usable from `file://`, with guarded Node exports.

## State and transport

Keep `vol` as total liquid slice area (px²), and add preallocated Float64 channel volumes R/G/B for each cell and each trapped cell. Their sum equals `vol` (or trapped total) within rounding error. All transport paths, including geometry closure/reopening, relaxation, supply, overflow and drain, move donor shares proportionally within the phase actually transferred. Never reconstruct color from a rendered pixel. Maintain per-channel attempted, admitted, unadmitted, overflowed and drained counters; melting/freezing have separate conversion counters. For each channel, admitted + melted = stored (including trapped) + overflowed + drained + frozen, with initial inventory included when nonzero. Do not count refused supply as admitted. Reset and drain cover trapped shares too.

Three pour-color checkboxes select equal shares of the inlet rate; default all selected, and prevent deselecting the last. The existing single-rate control still specifies total volume. Change existing direct-injection fixtures to assign shares explicitly; no implicit color fallback in the simulation.

## Mixability and density

Three independent mixable checkboxes, initially checked. Checked channels form one homogeneous phase with effective density 1000 kg/m³; each unchecked channel is its own phase, with R 2000, G 1000, B 500 kg/m³ — the grain palette's own values (dense red, normal water, buoyant blue), so 0.4 melt and freeze move between materials of one density family and a blue grain hangs neutral in blue liquid. At most three phases exist in a cell. Sort phases by effective density, heavy below light, with a fixed channel-order tie break. Split or merge phases immediately on a checkbox change without altering channel inventories; repeated toggles must not lose volume. This is an illustrative effective-density model: channel volume is conserved on toggling, not a physical equation of state. Keeping the miscible phase at a fixed 1000 also makes the all-mixable solver identical to the single-liquid baseline, which is the regression gate for every later change; a volume-weighted blend density is a possible later knob, not a default.

Extend the vertical fill/lower-first relaxation to exchange phases in density order, including a heavier phase above a lighter phase in a filled column. An unstable neighbour pair exchanges a bounded volume per pass — swap equal slice areas of the two phases across the face, capped by both availabilities and a fraction of cell capacity — so a light layer buried under a heavy one rises over a few ticks, quasi-statically and exactly per channel, never through a closed seam. Across horizontal faces use each phase's occupied vertical band and the existing sill/connected-mask constraints; transfer only the phase that can reach the sill and its proportional channel shares. Bound transfers by available phase volume and receiver capacity, and preserve positivity. Trapped liquid retains its composition and is sorted on reopening. Reuse preallocated per-phase scratch arrays; do not allocate in the step loop.

Publish per-phase vertical boundaries alongside the existing free surface. Apply grain buoyancy as `g * (1 - sum(rho_layer/rho_grain * submerged_fraction_in_layer))`, computing band fractions as differences of the existing circular-segment function. Preserve existing drag using total wetted fraction. Account for displacement in layer capacity with an overlap-weighted approximation consistent with the current grid; record the approximation and test displaced capacity for no negative inventory. When all colors are mixable, identical-density results must agree with the old single-liquid solver within numerical tolerance.

## Views

Use the existing mask/display sampling path, not per-texel geometry queries. Sample the phase at texel y, using its channel shares for color and retaining the existing surface and depth alpha. For a nonempty phase set `I[k] = 255 * v[k] / max(v[R], v[G], v[B])`; empty cells remain transparent. This dominant-normalized display preserves ratios while allowing R+G to be yellow and equal R+G+B to be white. It is a visualization, not a physical additive-light intensity or a measure of liquid volume.

A Color/Pigment radio is display-only. Color displays `I`. Pigment displays neutral gray with `L = clamp((765 - (I.R + I.G + I.B)) / 2, 0, 255)`, rounded to byte. For an RGB input of (80,80,80), the mapping clamps to 255; (255,255,0) maps to about 128 and (255,255,255) to 0. Note that normalized equal channel shares display as (255,255,255), not (80,80,80), and pure primaries map to white in Pigment mode. A separated pure band is therefore light in Pigment mode. View changes must never affect state or dynamics. Explain normalization and these consequences in Help.

## Slices and acceptance gates

1. **Channels and views.** Add channel accounting, proportional transfers, inlet choices and both views with all colors mixed. `node experiments/water-color-fixtures.cjs`: conservation per channel through all transfer, close/reopen, supply, overflow, drain and reset paths; nonnegative finite values; analytic mapping anchors above; toggle views without state change. Existing water state, buoyancy and rendering smoke fixtures remain green.
2. **Separated phases.** Add checkboxes, phase sorting, sill transport, layer display and buoyancy. Fixtures: red settles below green below blue in a closed connected column from any pouring order within a measured settling budget, a light layer poured under a heavy one reaches the top within the same budget, mixed phases retain their ratios, no transfer through a closed sill, split/merge and trapped reopen conserve each channel, and grain acceleration in known layer bands matches the analytic expression. All-mixable comparison against the original reference is within a stated tolerance.
3. **Scenarios and measurement.** Add `experiments/water-color-balance.cjs` and `experiments/water-color-picture.cjs`; record reproducible commands, seed, time step, hardware and numbers in `experiments/logs/`. Capture mixed and layered examples in both views, including a gate closure. Run existing water separation/gate/buoyancy and render smoke fixtures. Check repeated toggles and long-running closed-box channel residuals, as well as a wet-step and mask-rebuild timing comparison against the baseline.

## Balance and limits

Acceptance is measured, not assumed: publish max absolute and relative channel residuals and tolerances, closed-column ordering and settling time, mixed-only baseline deltas, and timing before/after. Baseline water observations include 0/24 floats entering a surface-level side pocket, gate capture of 4/4 floats and 6/6 dense grains, roughly 0.20 ms dry / 1.37 ms turning / 0.32 ms wet step and a 16 ms mask rebuild on the recorded machine; these are comparisons, not guarantees on other hardware. The grid has no resolved droplets, turbulence, interfacial tension, molecular diffusion or calibrated mixing kinetics. Layering and displacement are approximations, especially at narrow seams and under ceilings.
