# Rotary valve field

Open `index.html` directly in a browser to choose a prototype. No install, build, network access or server is required.

- `prototype/draft.md`: original specification.
- `prototype/comparison.md`: requirements comparison and solver limitations.
- `archive/0.1.1-plan.md`: archived initial combined-version design and acceptance gates.
- `0.2-plan.md`: `v0.2` specification — auto-rotate percentage, press-drag turning, player-tinted housings, Game mode, composable quest generator and bot competitors.
- `pressure-plan.md`: inlet boundaries, air backends, ramp calibration and validation limits.
- `water-plan.md`: phased plan for a water inlet, rising level and buoyancy, with falling droplets deferred.
- `prototype/shared/`: combined implementation. `geometry.js` is the single source of truth for housings, passages, seam apertures and clearance; `simulation.js` advances conserved grains through `sweep` with moving-wall contact and spatial bins; `game.js` manages Lab/Game modes, quests, hue-band matching and bots; `render.js` strokes those same primitives, paints player-tinted housings and batches grains.
- `experiments/`: Node validation and timing scripts (`geometry-check.cjs`, `geometry-fixtures.cjs`, `simulation-fixtures.cjs`, `gas-fixtures.cjs`, `air-fixtures.cjs`, `calibration-fixtures.cjs`, `game-fixtures.cjs`, `render-smoke.cjs`, `simulation-bench.cjs`, and `behavior-probe.cjs`).

Click any valve, press-drag around its rim (or horizontally across its hub), or focus the canvas and use arrow keys plus Enter/Space (Shift to reverse), to turn a selected valve by 90°. **Auto-rotate** sets the deterministic percentage of valves that schedule automatic quarter-turns (`90%` default in Lab mode, `20%` in Game mode). Rotation speed controls valves, not gravity. Static help lives in the **Help** drawer (`?`), which lists every shortcut. The **Place valve** drawer (`V`) is a brush for double-click (or `P` on the selected valve): pick a valve type, direction, and/or player case tint — each left unselected to keep the current one (click a selected button again to unselect) — copy any field valve's type and direction into the brush, or copy a type name (with the selected direction, when one is selected) to the clipboard as text for chat. Changing only the direction or case tint keeps grains; changing the type clears that cell.

Switch **Mode** (`G`) between **Lab** (free sandbox) and **Game**: a 4-player scoreboard (**You** plus three bots toggled by **Bot control**) and a live 5-goal board. Only one competitor can sign onto each goal. Signing spawns `1–2` player-tinted `'hollow single · direction ↑ N · 270° (ports N)'` goal bottles on the field; route grains from spouts (`Pour`) or steer air vents until the bottle satisfies its target for the hold window, then the bottle seals into a `'solid blank'` plug, awards points, and spawns a new procedural quest. Quests combine difficulty properties: wide vs narrow count ranges, large counts, exact counts (`1` or `7`), timers, `2`/`3`/`6`-band color groups (allowing circular-mean average hue inside the target band), and high/low air-pressure thresholds (`> x` or `< x`).

Air is controlled by the mouths in the two bands: every column has one inlet above the field (low) and one below it (high). Click a mouth to seal or open it, Shift-click to swap its polarity. **Grid gas** (default) shows a relative potential as a blue-to-red overlay and uses it to push grains; a packed pile changes the potential and slows the modeled flow. **Air particles** shows a cyan kinetic population that circulates from bottom sources to top vents and knocks grains about. **Air force** scales the push from 0 (grains behave as with air off) to 2 (updrafts can hold a grain). **Off** leaves the grains untouched. Switching modes does not reset grains or valve targets.

The field only exists as a **difference**: one open source and one open vent. A band with every mouth sealed is a dead end, so the grid reports no through-flow, carries no flow, and the overlay and legend disappear (the legend names the missing side) instead of flooding to one flat colour. Sealing mouths one by one still raises or lowers the field, so the last mouth of a band is the reference, not an off switch. A solved field also eases in over about a third of a second, so a click or a rotor turn reads as a front moving through the machine.

The grid legend displays **equivalent gauge Pa**, not a measured local pressure: it maps the normalized 0–1 field to an editable reference span. The default 0.7125 Pa span comes from a 20-second particle-wall impulse measurement in an idealized reference chamber, using 1 mm/px, 1 s per simulation second, 1 mg per mass unit and 10 mm depth. It does not change the simulation forces, establish atmospheric pressure, or make the grid a thermodynamic model. See `pressure-plan.md` for the assumptions, analytical seam checks and limitations. Run `node experiments/calibration-fixtures.cjs`, `node experiments/gas-fixtures.cjs`, `node experiments/air-fixtures.cjs`, and `node experiments/game-fixtures.cjs` for the checks; `experiments/logs/simulation-pressure.txt` contains earlier coupling and cost measurements.
