# Rotary valve field

Open `index.html` directly in a browser to choose a prototype. No install, build, network access or server is required.

- `prototype/draft.md`: original specification.
- `prototype/comparison.md`: requirements comparison and solver limitations.
- `archive/0.1.1-plan.md`: archived initial combined-version design and acceptance gates.
- `0.2-plan.md`: `v0.2` specification — auto-rotate percentage, press-drag turning, player-tinted housings, Game mode, composable quest generator and bot competitors.
- `pressure-plan.md`: inlet boundaries, air backends, ramp calibration and validation limits.
- `0.3-water-plan.md`: phased plan for a water inlet, rising level, buoyancy and separation, with falling droplets deferred.

Click any valve, press-drag around its rim (or horizontally across its hub), or focus the canvas and use arrow keys plus Enter/Space (Shift to reverse), to turn a selected valve by 90°. A press-drag is a quarter-turn dial: one quarter turn of the hand around the rim (or 52 px across the hub, after a little grip slack) buys one 90° detent, so small movements rubber-band instead of spinning the rotor; the overlay draws the arc under the hand, the detent the rotor is committed to, and the grip point. **Auto-rotate** sets the deterministic percentage of valves that schedule automatic quarter-turns (`90%` default in Lab mode, `7%` in Game mode, `5%` in Vent mode). Rotation speed controls valves, not gravity. Static help lives in the **Help** drawer (`?`), which lists every shortcut. The **Place valve** drawer (`V`) is a brush for double-click (or `P` on the selected valve): pick a valve type, direction, and/or player case tint — each left unselected to keep the current one (click a selected button again to unselect) — copy any field valve's type and direction into the brush, or copy a type name (with the selected direction, when one is selected) to the clipboard as text for chat. Changing only the direction or case tint keeps grains; changing the type clears that cell.

Switch **Mode** (`G`, cycling **Lab** → **Game** → **Vent**) to move between the free sandbox, a scoreboard and a live 5-goal board, and pressure regulation through prebuilt random-map routes. The competitor list is not fixed: **Add seat** / **Remove** add and drop seats mid-match up to five (two in Vent mode), your own seat always stays, and every seat has its own **bot** checkbox saying whether a bot or a person plays it and a difficulty that sets the bot's pace — **Calm 1 Hz**, **Brisk 1.5 Hz** or **Quick 2 Hz** map changes, one action per interval with jitter that only slows it — **Bot control** is only a pause over the seats that are bots at all. Dropping a seat renumbers the ones behind it, so case tints, goal bottles and signed quests follow the seat list instead of pointing at a color nobody holds, and any change re-deals what the list owns: Game mode deals the mouth bands again, Vent mode gives back its routed cells and builds routes for the new list. Scores stay with the player. A bot seat's mouths and valves ignore the mouse until its checkbox is unticked, and the status line says whose seat refused you. In Game mode the two mouth bands are dealt out instead of open to everyone: each competitor owns **two in** (source) mouths at the bottom and **two out** (vent) mouths at the top, each at an independent random column — `2 in` and `2 out` per player, cased in that player's color, with owner plates on both band edges — and only the owner can press them, so there is no sweeping either band. A player's four mouths always sit in four different columns, and none of their in mouths shares a column with one of their out mouths; the bands are otherwise independent, so a column can carry one player's source and another player's vent. Pressure quests keep their advertised threshold and place a four-way bottle where a possible valve route leads to a usable source or sink, regardless of ownership or column. Turn valves to connect that reservoir and isolate unwanted branches; signing does not build the route for you. Only one competitor can sign onto each goal. Signing spawns `1–2` player-tinted goal bottles on the field (grain quests use `'hollow single · direction ↑ N · 270° (ports N)'`, pressure quests use a four-way `'hollow cross'`); route grains from spouts (`Pour`) or steer air vents until the bottle satisfies its target for the hold window, then the bottle seals into a `'solid blank'` plug, awards points, and spawns a new procedural quest. Quests combine difficulty properties: wide vs narrow count ranges, large counts, exact counts (`1` or `7`), timers, `2`/`3`/`6`-band color groups (allowing circular-mean average hue inside the target band), and high/low air-pressure thresholds (`> x` or `< x`).

**Vent mode** is a pressure-regulation game for **one or two seats**; more players would share the field's pressure average. The same seeded random valve map is retained. Setup prebuilds a source/vent route from random boundary mouths through existing on-map valves to a player-tinted collector whose exact type is `hollow single · direction · random°`; each seat also gets a second random source and vent mouth, which are routed through extra branches where the existing valve topology permits. Route valves keep their randomly assigned types and are only oriented as needed to make the gas path; no straight-pipe columns or easier replacement valves are installed. A mouth the route search could not join is still the owner's to press, but the field marks it with an owner ring only — no stem or flow arrow, so it never reads as a pipe that steers the collector. Grains are drained, grid gas is enabled, and the collector and planned paths are locked in alignment; other valves selected by the `5%` auto-rotate setting continue on their normal schedule. Valve play is **turn based**: the highlighted seat turns exactly one unlocked valve by 90° (`Shift` for the other way; press-drag is off), which spends the turn at once and hands it to the other seat after a one second gap. A turn left unspent expires after ten seconds, and **Pass** hands it on immediately. Route valves and collectors stay locked; a bot spends its turn only while its band is slipping and only on a valve in its own collector's air pocket. A large readout beside the canvas labels live pressure and requested range, with a scale marking the target band and current pressure; each collector also keeps its field gauge. Hold pressure inside the band for `4 s` to score; narrower bands pay more, and the next band moves a step. Leaving Vent mode restores every changed valve's original type, chamber, orientation, tint, lock and owner, and returns the mouths to the lab.

Air is controlled by the mouths in two bands **outside** the 10×10 field: every column has one inlet above (low) and one below (high). In Lab mode click any mouth to seal or open it, Shift-click to swap its polarity; in Game mode only your own dealt mouths are there and an owned mouth keeps the polarity it was dealt. **Grid gas** (default) solves a conservative diffusion of relative pressure on a pipe network and shows it as a blue-to-red overlay; an open mouth holds its cells at exactly `1` or `0`, and air only crosses links that a small clearance disc can actually travel through. Every valve's passages are divided into cells in **the rotor's own unrotated coordinates**, while the short pipes in its casing stay fixed; a turn recomputes only the seam links between them, so a part-turned rotor throttles its ports continuously and carries its air with the passage instead of having the mouths repaint it. A packed pile raises the resistance of its cells and slows the modeled flow. **Air particles** shows a cyan kinetic population that circulates from bottom sources to top vents and knocks grains about. **Air force** scales the push from 0× (off) to 2×: with Grid gas selected, 1× balances gravity for a standard grain in the vertical vent test, while 2× gives net lift and affects smaller/lighter grains more. The visual calibration is available at [`experiments/air-scale.html`](experiments/air-scale.html). **Lubricant** (`0–2`, default `1`) reduces tangential grain-to-grain and grain-to-wall friction. The contact solver handles touching pairs before overlap develops and follows curved wall contours without injecting velocity. **Lubricant stress** (`0–0.5×`, default off) adds an optional deterministic micro-shake capped at half the previous kick amplitude for a stubborn jam. Switching modes does not reset grains or valve targets.

**Water** is a separate, off-by-default mode, and it is quasi-static: there is no jet, no slosh and no falling drop. **Vent open** pours slice area at **Rate** (`px²/s`) into the pocket under one fixed column; the water fills the passages it can reach up to the lowest open face it can spill over, so pockets perch at different levels and a sill only lets water across once the level reaches it. Fully sealed cavities admit nothing, a blocked mouth is counted as unadmitted, and a full system backs the excess out of the top as overflow. **Drain water** empties every pool and counts it as drained, with `admitted = stored + overflowed + drained` holding throughout. Grains displace water by the part of their disc that is under the waterline, which raises the level and pushes water into the neighbouring cells; the fill is drawn translucent behind the grains with its free surface stroked over it. Colour is density — red 2,000, amber 1,400, teal 1,000, pink 1,200, green 800, blue 500 kg/m³ against water at 1,000 — so a fully submerged grain accelerates at `g (1 − 1000/density)`: blue rises at one `g`, teal is weightless, red sinks at half gravity. A floating grain settles where its submerged fraction equals `density/1000`, so blue floats half under, green four fifths, and anything denser than water sinks. Submerged drag is bounded (`1 − exp(−6·f·h)` per step, so it can only remove speed) and sets how fast a grain reaches its terminal velocity, not how far it goes. Air pressure mode is parked while water is on, because there is no validated gas/water interface yet, and the player's mode comes back when water is switched off. Run `node experiments/water-fixtures.cjs` for materials, mass and the segment kernel, `water-state-fixtures.cjs` for components, sills, perched pools, split/merge, supply and two mask resolutions, `water-buoyancy-fixtures.cjs` for the force law, drag, the waterline and the display, and `water-separation.cjs` for the measured separation of a mixed bed (float recovery, sink recovery, cross contamination, and drag-versus-time balance). `water-picture.cjs` and `water-separation.cjs` write previews to `experiments/logs/`.

A mouth click or newly opened seam starts pressure exchange on the next simulation tick. Moving seams are refreshed on every fixed tick, so a fast rotor cannot pass an opening between 30 Hz updates; still fields settle at 30 Hz. Connected casing and rotor cells exchange pressure even when they have no route to an inlet. Closing every mouth does not repaint the field or add air: each isolated pocket keeps its total air, while pressure differences inside connected pockets gradually even out. Turning a rotor carries the air inside it and changes the seam links; closed rims do not leak. The overlay and its scale remain visible, and a turning rotor also stirs air inside its disc and just outside its open ports.

The grid legend displays **equivalent gauge Pa**, not a measured local pressure: it maps the normalized 0–1 pressure to an editable reference span. The default 0.7125 Pa span comes from a 20-second particle-wall impulse measurement in an idealized reference chamber, using 1 mm/px, 1 s per simulation second, 1 mg per mass unit and 10 mm depth. It does not change the simulation forces, establish atmospheric pressure, or make the grid a thermodynamic model. See `pressure-plan.md` for the pipe network, transient solver, analytical seam checks and limitations. Run `node experiments/pressure-propagation-fixtures.cjs`, `node experiments/calibration-fixtures.cjs`, `node experiments/gas-fixtures.cjs`, `node experiments/air-fixtures.cjs`, and `node experiments/game-fixtures.cjs` for the checks; `experiments/logs/grid-gas-propagation.txt` and `experiments/logs/grid-gas-cleanup-0.2.txt` contain the latest gas-propagation and cost measurements. Run `node experiments/vent-fixtures.cjs` for Vent-mode route, collector, seat and teardown invariants and `node experiments/vent-balance.cjs` for route coverage, pressure reach and exact restore across seeds.
