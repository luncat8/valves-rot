Click any valve, press-drag around its rim (or horizontally across its hub), or focus the canvas and use arrow keys plus Enter/Space (Shift to reverse), to turn a selected valve by 90°. Selection is explicit and sticky: a clicked valve stays selected while the cursor moves away (a faint ring marks the hovered cell without committing), **Shift-click** or a still ~½ s press in Lab adds or removes cells from a multi-selection without turning anything, clicking empty space clears it, and the Heat panel carries **Select all** / **Unselect** buttons; in Game and Vent modes Shift-click keeps its reverse-turn meaning. A press-drag is a quarter-turn dial: one quarter turn of the hand around the rim (or 52 px across the hub, after a little grip slack) buys one 90° detent, so small movements rubber-band instead of spinning the rotor; the overlay draws the arc under the hand, the detent the rotor is committed to, and the grip point. **Auto-rotate** sets the deterministic percentage of valves that schedule automatic quarter-turns (`90%` default in Lab mode, `7%` in Game mode, `5%` in Vent mode). Rotation speed controls valves, not gravity. Static help lives in the **Help** drawer (`?`), which lists every shortcut. The **Place valve** drawer (`V`) is a brush for double-click (or `P` on the selected valve): pick a valve type — including the two dual-channel 2+2 solid valves, `solid bends` (`(><)`, pairing adjacent ports `E–N` & `S–W` or `S–E` & `W–N`) and `solid overpass` (`|` / `-`, bridging opposite ports `E–W` over `S–N` or `S–N` over `W–E` without mixing grains, gas, or water) — direction, and/or player case tint, each left unselected to keep the current one (click a selected button again to unselect), copy any field valve's type and direction into the brush, or copy a type name (with the selected direction, when one is selected) to the clipboard as text for chat. Changing only the direction or case tint keeps grains; changing the type clears that cell.

Switch **Mode** (`G`, cycling **Lab** → **Game** → **Vent**) to move between the free sandbox, a scoreboard and a live 5-goal board, and pressure regulation through prebuilt random-map routes. The competitor list is not fixed: **Add seat** / **Remove** add and drop seats mid-match up to five (two in Vent mode), your own seat always stays, and every seat has its own **bot** checkbox saying whether a bot or a person plays it and a difficulty that sets the bot's pace — **Calm 1 Hz**, **Brisk 1.5 Hz** or **Quick 2 Hz** map changes, one action per interval with jitter that only slows it — **Bot control** is only a pause over the seats that are bots at all. Dropping a seat renumbers the ones behind it, so case tints, goal bottles and signed quests follow the seat list instead of pointing at a color nobody holds, and any change re-deals what the list owns: Game mode deals the mouth bands again, Vent mode gives back its routed cells and builds routes for the new list. Scores stay with the player. A bot seat's mouths and valves ignore the mouse until its checkbox is unticked, and the status line says whose seat refused you. In Game mode the two mouth bands are dealt out instead of open to everyone: each competitor owns **two in** (source) mouths at the bottom and **two out** (vent) mouths at the top, each at an independent random column — `2 in` and `2 out` per player, cased in that player's color, with owner plates on both band edges — and only the owner can press them, so there is no sweeping either band. A player's four mouths always sit in four different columns, and none of their in mouths shares a column with one of their out mouths; the bands are otherwise independent, so a column can carry one player's source and another player's vent. Pressure quests keep their advertised threshold and place a four-way bottle where a possible valve route leads to a usable source or sink, regardless of ownership or column. Turn valves to connect that reservoir and isolate unwanted branches; signing does not build the route for you. Only one competitor can sign onto each goal. Signing spawns `1–2` player-tinted goal bottles on the field (grain quests use `'hollow single · direction ↑ N · 270° (ports N)'`, pressure quests use a four-way `'hollow cross'`); route grains from spouts (`Pour`) or steer air vents until the bottle satisfies its target for the hold window, then the bottle seals into a `'solid blank'` plug, awards points, and spawns a new procedural quest. Quests combine difficulty properties: wide vs narrow count ranges, large counts, exact counts (`1` or `7`), timers, `2`/`3`/`6`-band color groups (allowing circular-mean average hue inside the target band), and high/low air-pressure thresholds (`> x` or `< x`).

**Vent mode** is a pressure-regulation game for **one or two seats**; more players would share the field's pressure average. The same seeded random valve map is retained. Setup prebuilds a source/vent route from random boundary mouths through existing on-map valves to a player-tinted collector whose exact type is `hollow single · direction · random°`; each seat also gets a second random source and vent mouth, which are routed through extra branches where the existing valve topology permits. Route valves keep their randomly assigned types and are only oriented as needed to make the gas path; no straight-pipe columns or easier replacement valves are installed. A mouth the route search could not join is still the owner's to press, but the field marks it with an owner ring only — no stem or flow arrow, so it never reads as a pipe that steers the collector. Grains are drained, grid gas is enabled, and the collector and planned paths are locked in alignment; other valves selected by the `5%` auto-rotate setting continue on their normal schedule. Valve play is **turn based**: the highlighted seat turns exactly one unlocked valve by 90° (`Shift` for the other way; press-drag is off), which spends the turn at once and hands it to the other seat after a one second gap. A turn left unspent expires after ten seconds, and **Pass** hands it on immediately. Route valves and collectors stay locked; a bot spends its turn only while its band is slipping and only on a valve in its own collector's air pocket. A large readout beside the canvas labels live pressure and requested range, with a scale marking the target band and current pressure; each collector also keeps its field gauge. Hold pressure inside the band for `4 s` to score; narrower bands pay more, and the next band moves a step. Leaving Vent mode restores every changed valve's original type, chamber, orientation, tint, lock and owner, and returns the mouths to the lab.

Air is controlled by the mouths in two bands **outside** the 10×10 field: every column has one inlet above (low) and one below (high). In Lab mode click any mouth to seal or open it, Shift-click to swap its polarity; in Game mode only your own dealt mouths are there and an owned mouth keeps the polarity it was dealt. **Grid gas** (default) solves a conservative diffusion of relative pressure on a pipe network and shows it as a blue-to-red overlay; an open mouth holds its cells at exactly `1` or `0`, and air only crosses links that a small clearance disc can actually travel through. Every valve's passages are divided into cells in **the rotor's own unrotated coordinates**, while the short pipes in its casing stay fixed; a turn recomputes only the seam links between them, so a part-turned rotor throttles its ports continuously and carries its air with the passage instead of having the mouths repaint it. A packed pile raises the resistance of its cells and slows the modeled flow. **Air particles** shows a cyan kinetic population that circulates from bottom sources to top vents and knocks grains about. **Air force** scales the push from 0× (off) to 2×: with Grid gas selected, 1× balances gravity for the neutral 2.7 px grain (the reference mass) in the vertical vent test, while 2× gives it net lift and lighter grains rise faster, because gas drag scales inversely with grain mass. The visual calibration is available at [`experiments/air-scale.html`](experiments/air-scale.html), with buoyant-blue, neutral and dense-red lanes. **Lubricant** (`0–2`, default `1`) reduces tangential grain-to-grain and grain-to-wall friction. The contact solver handles touching pairs before overlap develops and follows curved wall contours without injecting velocity. **Lubricant stress** (`0–0.5×`, default off) adds an optional deterministic micro-shake capped at half the previous kick amplitude for a stubborn jam. Switching modes does not reset grains or valve targets.

**Water** is a separate, off-by-default mode, and it is quasi-static: there is no jet, no slosh and no falling drop. The **input source vents** are one tab per column in the strip above the top air mouths, and each is its own source: it pours slice area at its own **Rate** (`px²/s`) into the pocket under its column, so ten different liquids can pour at once. Click a tab to make it the **current vent** — the one the global water controls edit; **slide left/right** on it to sweep its pour colour around the RGB wheel (one tab width is a full revolution, and the tab shows the spectrum and cursor while you drag); **slide up/down** to set its rate from `0` to the global Rate ceiling; **double-click** for a colour picker, whose picked colour becomes proportional channel shares (pure black is refused — it carries no ink ratio — and brightness normalizes away as it always does in the fill); **long-click** (hold ~½ s) to open or shut it, which a sealed cross on a dimmed tab marks. The global **Column** slider is a brush: moving it stamps the current vent's settings onto every vent it passes, so setting one vent red and sweeping the column from 1 to 10 turns the whole row red — useful for setting up many vents. The water fills the passages it can reach up to the lowest open face it can spill over, so pockets perch at different levels and a sill only lets water across once the level reaches it. Fully sealed cavities admit nothing, a blocked mouth is counted as unadmitted, and a full system backs the excess out of the top as overflow. **Drain water** empties every pool and counts it as drained, with `admitted = stored + overflowed + drained` holding throughout. Grains displace water by the part of their disc that is under the waterline, which raises the level and pushes water into the neighbouring cells; the fill is drawn translucent behind the grains with its free surface stroked over it. Colour is density — red 2,000, amber 1,400, teal 1,000, pink 1,200, green 800, blue 500 kg/m³ against water at 1,000 — so a fully submerged grain accelerates at `g (1 − 1000/density)`: blue rises at one `g`, teal is weightless, red sinks at half gravity. A floating grain settles where its submerged fraction equals `density/1000`, so blue floats half under, green four fifths, and anything denser than water sinks. Submerged drag is bounded (`1 − exp(−6·f·h)` per step, so it can only remove speed) and sets how fast a grain reaches its terminal velocity, not how far it goes. Air pressure mode is parked while water is on, because there is no validated gas/water interface yet, and the player's mode comes back when water is switched off. A float can be lifted but not pushed: water gives it no sideways force, so an open pocket at the surface level never takes one (measured: 0 of 24 in 14 s). The capture is vertical instead. Build a one-chamber-wide shaft with a gate valve in it, fill to just above the gate, pour the mixture down the shaft, and the dense grains sink to the pool at the bottom while the floats stay on the surface. Raise the last stretch slowly — the surface must not outrun the lightest float, whose net buoyancy caps it near 19 px/s — then turn the gate a quarter turn shut under the layer and **Drain water**. The gate is a wall, so the layer stays above it and the dense bed below it: measured 4 of 4 floats captured, 6 of 6 dense grains left below, no dense grain above the gate, and no grain lost to the gate or the drain. A hole in the floor would take the grains with the water, which is why the trap is emptied from above. Liquid carries **three conserved colour channels**, R, G and B. Every transfer — vertical fill, sill spill, the push out of a closing cell, a trapped return, supply, overflow, drain — moves the donor's channels in proportion through one `move(area)` primitive, so no motion path can carry one colour and lose another, and `chInitial + chAdmitted = stored + overflowed + drained` holds per channel rather than only in total. Each cell's `vol` is a cache of its channel sum, and the fixtures publish the residual instead of assuming it. Every split hands its rounding remainder to the largest channel and clamps each share to what that channel holds, so no channel can be driven negative — the closed-box measurement scans all of them after every step. The **R**/**G**/**B** pour boxes split the current vent's total **Rate** equally among the selected colours (the last one cannot be switched off, and the module refuses an empty mask rather than inventing a default), so the selection changes the mix, not the delivered volume; a hue-picked vent mirrors back as every channel it uses, and editing from the boxes snaps it to the equal-share mix. The fill normalizes each cell's channels so the strongest is 255: equal shares read white, R+G reads yellow, and a diluted colour keeps its hue instead of fading. That triple is painted at a fixed 55% shade, because white over pale porcelain would otherwise read as a brighter wall than as liquid. **Pigment** view paints one neutral level instead, `L = clamp((765 − (R + G + B))/2, 0, 255)`, from the same triple: white reads black, (80,80,80) reads full light, (255,255,0) reads about half, and a pure primary reads light. Both are views; neither touches the water. **Mix** R/G/B groups the channels into density phases — red 2,000, green 1,000, blue 500 — and phases that do not mix settle into layers: vertical swaps sort each column while lateral trades break the lock-exchange freeze, so an inverted R/G/B stack orders in about a second and a wide basin in about five. A grain's buoyancy is summed over the bands its disc crosses, so it reads the layer it sits in (teal sinks in red, floats in blue), and the fill paints each layer's own triple with the splits at the published band boundaries. Behind a shut gate the layers hold bit-exact. Run `node experiments/water-color-fixtures.cjs` for the channel ledger, proportional transport, pour shares and both views.
**Heat** is an illustrative thermal field on the water grid, off by default: one total enthalpy per cell covers metal, liquid and grains, and temperature is derived from it, so melting and freezing plateaus are real buffers. Enabling Heat enables Water with every vent shut; **Power** takes a signed value (heater or cooler badge) and edits **every selected valve at once** — click selects one, Shift-click or a long press builds a multi-selection, Select all / Unselect set or clear the field, and the readout counts the valves (the slider stays disabled and prompts "select a valve" until one is selected) and its scale is ±10000 E/s, tuned against the melt work at the retuned table — the sensible heats (metal, liquid, grains) and the latent book are half the 0.4 draft values, which keeps the melt-vs-freeze work ratio per channel unchanged, so the melt leg sets the pace: at ±1500 E/s a frozen pool never melts back inside the 150 s measurement window, and beyond the shipped scale the melt is placement-bound, not power-bound. **Capacity** and **Latent** rescale the field's sensible heats and latent book live (×0.25–×2 against the shipped table, every cell at once, through the same validating setParams the fixtures use), so the heater's kick and the plateaus' weight can be tuned by hand. **Ambient** sets the room behind the outer-ring Newton loss and rebases the field at once when typed so the change is visible immediately, and **Temperature view** paints the whole machine on a fixed 0–240 °C scale while the liquid sheet dims. Materials melt into the colour channels by mass (red→R, amber→½R½G, teal→½G½B, pink→½R½B, green→G, blue→B) at the highest plateau among their channels, and freezing pays channels back as primary grains, highest freeze point first, where a channel's freeze point is the lower of its plateau (R 120, G 100, B 80 °C defaults) and the room temperature — so supply spawned at the room never freezes on arrival, a hot melt refreezes as it cools to the room, and a cooler, or a room below the plateau, still freezes a pool — sub-grain remainders stay liquid pinned at their freeze point, blocked placements defer without consuming anything, and every liquid move carries its enthalpy so the ledger `stored = opening + sources + room + material` closes through melts, freezes, flows and drains. The status line reports `heat X °C mean · room Y °C · src · net · mat · res` so the room setting and the cumulative room transfer read separately. A mouth click or newly opened seam starts pressure exchange on the next simulation tick. Moving seams are refreshed on every fixed tick, so a fast rotor cannot pass an opening between 30 Hz updates; still fields settle at 30 Hz. Connected casing and rotor cells exchange pressure even when they have no route to an inlet. Closing every mouth does not repaint the field or add air: each isolated pocket keeps its total air, while pressure differences inside connected pockets gradually even out. Turning a rotor carries the air inside it and changes the seam links; closed rims do not leak. The overlay and its scale remain visible, and a turning rotor also stirs air inside its disc and just outside its open ports.

The grid legend displays **equivalent gauge Pa**, not a measured local pressure: it maps the normalized 0–1 pressure to an editable reference span. The default 0.7125 Pa span comes from a 20-second particle-wall impulse measurement in an idealized reference chamber, using 1 mm/px, 1 s per simulation second, 1 mg per mass unit and 10 mm depth. It does not change the simulation forces, establish atmospheric pressure, or make the grid a thermodynamic model.
