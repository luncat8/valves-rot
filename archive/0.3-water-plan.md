# Water level and buoyancy plan

## Goal and scope

Add a water inlet (vent/spout) above the field. Opening it supplies water at a controlled rate and raises the water level in reachable passages. The purpose is to make separation of mixed grains possible: grains have distinct material densities and masses, so water can float some while others sink into different collection regions. The first implementation shows a changing water level, **not falling drops or a waterfall**. Falling water is a separate, deferred stage.

Keep the existing file-friendly, fixed-step simulation, conserved grains, shared geometry and preallocated hot-path buffers. Water is separate from the current air inlet controls and from the grid's relative pressure.

## Physical conventions

- Use one explicit length, simulation-time, mass and out-of-plane depth configuration for all physical conversions. The current air-ramp reference uses 1 mm/px, 1 s/simulation-second, 1 mg/mass unit and 10 mm depth. Its editable Pa span is only a display scale; changing that span must not change water or grain physics.
- A grain is an extruded circular disc in the 2-D slice: `V = π (r_px × metres_per_px)² × depth`, `m = grain_density × V`. Set water density to 1,000 kg/m³ by default; provide deterministic grain material types with densities below, equal to and above water. Density is independent of color and radius, even if colors help identify materials.
- The current gravity is 560 px/s², or 0.56 m/s² under the air reference scales, not Earth gravity. Preserve it explicitly for the initial model, or introduce a shared time/length calibration before offering Earth gravity. If the time scale changes, recompute the air reference, supply rates and all other SI conversions consistently.
- Introduce shared grain mass and inverse-mass arrays. The current grain-pair solver assumes equal masses, kinetic-air contacts use `r²`, and grid drag treats grains as unit mass; make all three consistent, including gas reaction momentum and every buffer compaction, insertion, purge, reset and replacement path. Changing density must not silently alter radius. Unequal-radius dry collisions will change, and air coupling must be rechecked because its particle and cell masses are tuned in simulation units.

## Grain identity and separation (shared with air)

Implement this shared foundation before water coupling, not as a purely cosmetic palette change. Today the six colors are independent labels, the pair solver assumes equal masses, particle air uses `r²`, and grid air uses a radius-based reference; merely recoloring grains would not separate them by material.

- Preserve the six existing palette indices and hue-based Game quests. Make each index a stable material identity with a documented, visible legend, rather than infer physics from rendered RGB. Suggested densities for indices 0–5 (red, amber, teal, blue, pink, green) are respectively 2,000, 1,400, 1,000, 500, 1,200 and 800 kg/m³. Red is dense, blue is buoyant, and the others bracket water; adjust values after measuring separation and game balance. Color identifies density, not mass: a larger blue grain can outweigh a smaller red grain. Radius is visible size, and mass follows density × disc volume. Do not encode mass as red and density as blue simultaneously: with variable size those quantities are not independent, RGB mixtures are ambiguous and existing quests depend on the palette.
- Use the same deterministic mapping for pour, fill, reset, explicit color injection and every mode; store or derive density from the material index, and compute mass and inverse mass from the actual radius. Never assign material from a random render color after creation. Existing same-color fill and color quests still work; document that their materials now have different behavior. Keep radius independent of material so equal-size materials can be compared.
- For air, use the same grain masses in grain-grain contacts, moving-wall contacts where applicable, particle-air impulses and grid-air coupling/reaction. Air resistance depends on exposed size and relative velocity as well as mass; do not simply make force proportional to color or promise that the current empirical grid-gas model is physically calibrated. Tune and document the approximate air response without claiming the Pa-equivalent display calibrates drag. Ensure air-off and water-off are valid dry baselines.
- Provide a reproducible separation scenario: a mixed, equal-radius population containing at least the blue light and red dense materials, a controllable water inlet and accessible high and low collection regions. Once buoyancy has time to act, route and count the floating and sinking groups separately; report recovered fractions and cross-contamination, not just different-looking motion. A comparable air-only scenario should use controlled airflow and show reproducible differential transport, with no guarantee that every geometry separates every pair. Avoid relying on color alone: expose material/density values in the legend and use counts by material.
- Test equal-size materials with different density, equal-density grains with different radii, and equal-mass grains of different sizes. Verify deterministic assignments and identity through insertion, compaction, drain and valve replacement, momentum exchange and conservation in both air backends, and unchanged quest color classification. Run fixed-seed air and water separation measurements and inspect whether gameplay needs rebalancing; publish thresholds based on measured baseline rather than inventing them before implementation.

## Implementation order (first slices)

Do not start with rendering or a global water level: the risky part is identity
and connectivity, and both are testable in Node without a browser.

1. **Material/mass foundation (this slice).** Palette index = material;
   `MATERIAL_DENSITY = [2000, 1400, 1000, 500, 1200, 800]` kg/m³ for indices
   0–5. In solver units `mass = (density / 1000) × r²` and `invMass = 1/mass`,
   so the existing air tuning (which already used `r²`, reference
   `2.7²`) is unchanged for the 1,000 kg/m³ material and only density moves
   the others. Preallocated `mass`/`invMass` arrays are written by `addGrain`
   and copied by every compaction path (`compact`, `clearValve`). Grain–grain
   normal/friction impulses are mass-weighted only on impacts (restitution
   branch, inverse-mass shares clamped to 2:1); resting support and the
   positional split stay equal — full shares squeeze wall-pinned light grains
   out of a settled mixed bed (measured: sealed-bed mean speed 22 px/s vs
   <5 px/s limit in `simulation-fixtures`).
   walls stay infinite-mass. Both air backends use `sim.mass`, never `r²`
   re-derived from color. Export the circular-segment submerged-fraction
   helper now so Phase 1 buoyancy has an analytically tested kernel.
2. **Water state without rendering.** One global-but-component-aware volume
   model on a coarse passability grid derived from shared geometry (not the
   gas graph), with spill sills, counters (`attempted/admitted/stored/
   overflowed/drained`) and split/merge conservation fixtures.

   Concrete model (`water.js`): a regular world grid (default 6 px, resolution
   is a create parameter so fixtures can compare two). Each cell stores its
   passable area plus a vertical extent and is modeled as an equal-area
   rectangle, so a volume maps to a horizontal free surface. Water is a
   per-cell volume (slice area, px²; uniform depth factors out) — the persistent
   state — and the free surface is derived. Faces are sampled from the shared
   `airClearance` predicate: a vertical (N/S) face joins two cells into one
   column; a horizontal (E/W) face records the deepest open point as its spill
   sill. A bounded Gauss–Seidel relaxation moves water from higher surface to
   lower, a source never draining below its face sill, which yields perched
   pools and sill spill without a global level. Per-cell state makes rotor
   split/merge conserve volume by construction; a closing wall displaces water
   into adjacent void and routes it through open faces only, holding any
   unreachable remainder as rotor-carried `trapped` volume returned on reopen.
   Rebuilds are cached per valve angle and layout revision, stamped per cell and
   per face so a turned rotor re-samples only its own block; the mask clock is
   15 Hz while the relaxation still runs every step. A grain's displaced area is
   spread over the cells that the submerged part of its disc actually covers
   (overlap-weighted), never loaded on the cell of its centre: one cell is 6 px
   and a grain up to 6.2 px across, so a centre-cell load makes the capacity, and
   with it the waterline a float feels, step whenever the grain crosses an edge.
   Counters are slice areas with `admitted = stored + overflowed + drained`,
   `attempted = admitted + unadmitted`; a metal mouth is blocked (unadmitted)
   while a full open mouth backs excess out the top (overflow). Water is off by
   default so the dry baseline is untouched.
3. **Buoyancy + display.** Segment-area force from the *published waterline,
   not a cell's own surface*: each vertical run is filled from its floor so a
   grain under a pool reads the pool's level (a per-cell surface makes a lone
   float oscillate as its own displacement empties its cell). Submerged drag is
   bounded (`1 − exp(−rate·h)` style, no energy injection); air is forced Off
   while water is enabled and the previous mode is restored when it goes off.
   The fill is drawn translucent behind the grains, with the free surface stroked
   on top of it, on the *mask's own sample grid* (2 px at the default cell): a
   texel's passability is one array read instead of a per-texel geometry query.
   That query cost 4.9 ms a frame and repeated what the mask had already sampled;
   the whole display pass is now 1.3 ms and slightly finer. Vent on/off, the vent
   *column*, rate, drain and a volume/pool readout complete the controls.
   Clearing the published levels is a one-shot on the way out of water mode, not
   a per-step fill of two grid-sized arrays.
4. **Separation scenario + balance.** A fixed layout — a vent shaft over a
   three-wide basin — filled by delivered volume, then a mixed bed of every
   material dropped into its two bottom pockets. Counts per material into the
   upper and lower halves of the pool (from the solver's own waterline and floor)
   give recovered fraction, cross contamination and upper-half purity; the same
   bed runs with water off as the baseline, and a lone float per material checks
   that the equilibrium is the density ratio rather than a crowd effect.
   `experiments/water-separation.cjs` prints the table and the recovery curve and
   asserts the thresholds measured below; the field is shared with the balance
   sweep through `experiments/water-scenario.cjs`.
5. **Float capture (this slice).** Water has no velocity, so a float moves only
   vertically: it can be *lifted*, not pushed sideways. The capture is therefore
   vertical — a one-chamber-wide shaft, the mixture poured in at the top, dense
   grains sinking to the pool below, floats staying on the surface above a gate
   valve; the gate is turned a quarter turn shut under the layer and the water is
   drained through **Drain water**, leaving the floats in the trap and the dense
   bed on the pool floor. `experiments/water-gate.cjs` runs the sequence, prints a
   per-material above/bore/pool table, asserts the layer is captured with zero
   cross contamination and no lost grain, and runs the negative control: an open
   side pocket at the surface level takes 0 of 24 floats in 14 s. The same script
   with `--floor` loads the mixture into the empty chamber before the fill, which
   is the harder case and is reported, not asserted as the mechanic.

Slice 1 changes dry collisions between different materials by design; same-
material, equal-radius behaviour must stay bit-close to the old solver.

### Measured balance (seeded basin, six materials, 72 grains)

Delivered with `WATER_DRAG = 6 /s` and equal radii `2.7 px`. Recovery reaches
100% for both groups with no cross contamination; the slow tail is the lightest
useful float, because net buoyancy `0.2 g` at `6 /s` drag caps `green` at
~19 px/s and it needs ~6 s to cross a 110 px pool.

`experiments/water-balance.cjs` is the table: it sweeps `sim.waterDrag` through
`S.setWaterDrag` on the separation field and the tight float pool, and asserts
the criteria below, so the balance is re-measurable rather than a remembered
number. Worst float wander is one float, radii 2.3 / 2.7 / 3.0 px, 10 s each, in
a 56 px chamber holding 260 px² — a pool shallower than the grain is tall.

| drag | 90% recovered at | final | float | sink | worst float wander |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 /s | 7 s | 90% | 100% | 83% | 5.0 px |
| 4 /s | 5 s | 100% | 100% | 100% | 4.1 px |
| 6 /s | 7 s | 100% | 100% | 100% | 2.3 px |
| 12 /s | never | 82% | 100% | 69% | 0.1 px |

Too little drag lets the bed churn and leaves dense grains stranded above the
midline; too much drag brakes them before they reach the floor at all, which is
what the tempting 0.1 px wander costs. `6 /s` is kept: both groups recover fully
and the float is the steadiest that still separates. Densities stay at
`[2000, 1400, 1000, 500, 1200, 800]`, which reads as three groups — floats at
half and four fifths submerged, a neutral material that hangs, and sinks at
quarter, half and seven tenths of gravity.

### Measured capture (one-wide shaft, gate at row 6, six materials poured in)

| | floats above the gate | dense below | dense strayed up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| layer lifted, gate shut, dry | **4 / 4** | **6 / 6** | **0** |
| same mixture dropped on the floor first (`--floor`) | 3 / 4 | 6 / 6 | 0 |

The poured run is the mechanic: fill to just above the gate, pour the mixture
down the shaft, let it settle, raise the last 45 px at `250 px²/s` so the surface
does not outrun the lightest float, shut the gate, drain. Twelve grains in, twelve
grains out, none lost to the gate or the drain. The floor run is the measured
limit, not a second recipe: a float that starts under the pile, in a shaft two
grains wide, is jammed, and one of the four ends pinned under a flat arm ceiling.

## Phase 1 — inlet, levels and buoyancy

### Water supply and state

- Provide one distinct top water vent, an on/off control, a column control so the pour can be aimed at a shaft rather than the middle of the field, a supply rate `Q` in m³/s, and a clear/drain-water action. The existing air mouths and grain Pour/Drain keep their own meanings. The water outlet is closed by default, so supplying water can increase the level; any optional water drain and overflow have explicit counters.
- Attempt `Q × dt` while the vent is open; record admitted and unadmitted supply separately. Track stored, overflowed and drained volume, with `admitted = stored + overflowed + drained` and `attempted = admitted + unadmitted`. Pause and single-step use simulation time, not wall-clock time. A blocked or full inlet never deposits water behind a wall.
- Use the shared geometry and a water-specific passability mask, with closed faces and true seam apertures. The current grid-gas network is designed for relative gas potential, not water volumes or hydrostatic connectivity; do not reuse its pressure values as a water-connectivity test. Unresolved sub-cell openings may conservatively remain closed, with their resolution stated.
- Represent water as volume and horizontal free-surface level(s), not water particles. Fill and equilibrate communicating reachable voids as a quasi-static approximation. Keep separate volumes and levels for isolated pockets; splitting or joining components as rotors move must preserve volume rather than teleporting it through closed seams. Retain or explicitly account for water displaced by moving walls.
- Connectivity alone is not enough to equalize every connected void: an elevated connecting passage can prevent transfer until water reaches its sill. Model gravity-dependent spill thresholds and retain perched pools; only equilibrate volumes that can actually exchange at the current levels. A dry chamber below an open inlet may receive water quasi-statically along an unobstructed downhill path, without representing the falling stream. Document the resolution and approximations of this routing.
- Compute water level from accessible void volume minus the submerged volume of grains. Handle fully filled compartments, displacement, overflow and blocked inlets explicitly. No hidden source, loss, or instant filling of a sealed dry chamber. The quasi-static approximation does not model jets, sloshing, surface tension or trapped-air compression.

### Grain coupling and display

- For each grain, compute the submerged circular-segment area below its local horizontal water surface, clamped from zero to the full disc area. Multiply by depth to get displaced volume; apply upward `F_b = water_density × gravity × displaced_volume` and gravity using the grain's actual mass. With screen y increasing downward, a fully submerged grain's vertical acceleration is `g × (1 − water_density / grain_density)`.
- Use a bounded, timestep-stable submerged drag toward the phase-1 stationary water; scale it by the submerged fraction and document it as an approximation. It should settle a floating grain near its equilibrium waterline without injecting energy. Dry grains and water-off mode must not receive water forces.
- Draw a translucent water fill and horizontal waterline only inside wetted air passages, behind grains and without painting over metal; the surface line is stroked over the fill so a level reads behind a bright grain. Show the top vent, level/volume and inlet state. Water volume transfers quasi-statically from the vent into the modeled connected pool: do not spawn, animate or imply individual falling drops in this phase.
- Avoid claiming a two-phase air solver. Initially require air pressure mode Off while water mode is enabled, including when its vent is closed but water remains; preserve the user's prior selection and restore it when water mode is disabled. This avoids air particles and the porous gas field occupying submerged cells until a validated gas/water interface exists. Make the restriction visible in the controls.

### Acceptance

- Empty, half-submerged and fully submerged disc areas match the analytical circular segment. With drag disabled in a free chamber, light, neutral and heavy equal-size grains accelerate upward, approximately zero, and downward respectively; floating equilibrium has submerged fraction `grain_density / water_density`.
- Check different radii and densities, mass-weighted pair/air impulses, momentum bookkeeping, and per-grain arrays through drain, compaction, reset and valve replacement. With water and air off, retain dry free-fall and equal-radius, equal-density collision regressions; different masses or radii intentionally change collision responses. Preserve grain conservation in every mode.
- At two mask resolutions, test open, narrowing and closed seams, isolated pockets, high sills and perched pools, component split/merge during turns, grain and wall displacement, inlet blockage, overflow and drain. Admitted volume equals stored plus drained/overflowed (within a stated discretization tolerance), with no wetting through a sealed seam.
- Check fixed-step equivalence, pause/single-step, controls and mode restoration, rendering alignment, no new per-frame allocations, and timings at representative grain counts. Document the waterline model's quasi-static and pressure limitations.
- Capture: a mixture poured into a one-wide flooded shaft leaves every float above a gate that is then shut, with no dense grain above it and no grain lost to the gate or the drain; an open pocket at the surface level takes none. The display draws only texels the mask admits and never paints a wall.

## Known limits of the quasi-static level

- Per-cell towers are equal-area rectangles, so a pool's published level is a
  piecewise-linear function of volume with breakpoints at cell boundaries, and
  neighbouring pockets joined only by a mid-height tube can sit 2–3 px apart.
  With the overlap-weighted displacement a float spanning cells settles within
  0.5 px in a normal chamber; in a pool not much larger than the grain's own
  displacement (~260 px² in a 28 px chamber) it still bobs ~2 px at ~1 Hz, which
  is the physical level feedback of a float in a vessel it nearly fills, damped
  but not removed. It cannot be averaged away: the tight case is a *single*
  column, so averaging over a connected run of columns would not touch it. Only a
  flow model that gives the water mass would.
- Water has no velocity, so it cannot carry a grain sideways. Separation is
  stratification in place, and a float can be lifted but not pushed: an open side
  pocket at the surface level takes none of them (0 of 24 floats in 14 s). A
  layer is captured by cutting the column *under* it, not by skimming sideways.
- A float can be pinned under a flat ceiling: a rotor arm is a 13.8 px tube with
  a horizontal roof, so a float that drifts into an arm has nothing that can push
  it back to the axis. That is why the captured run pours the mixture down the
  shaft — a float that starts under a pile in a two-grain-wide tube is jammed
  (measured 3 of 4 escape, one pinned under an arm).
- The published level is per vertical run, so a dead-end side arm can report a
  lower level than the pool it hangs off. A float there reads a fraction below
  1.0 and sits still, which matches where it physically is (under the arm roof),
  even though the reason is the run model rather than the roof.
- A hole in the floor drains grains with the water: opening a bottom port to
  empty the lower half of a split pool removed 20 of 24 grains in one run. Water
  leaves through geometry a grain cannot pass, or the pool keeps its water; the
  trap therefore finishes with **Drain water**, and the gate is a wall, so the
  layer stays where the water left it.

## Deferred — falling water

Consider a bounded, preallocated particle stream from the top vent for visibly falling water, splashes and transient delivery. Compare it with a cheaper visual sheet before selecting a model. If particles carry volume, count that volume once while in flight, sweep them against the actual valve/seam geometry, and merge it into the correct pool on impact. Conservation becomes `admitted = stored + in flight + drained + overflowed`; changing the particle budget or timestep must not change the delivered volume. Do not claim that visual droplets alone simulate incompressible fluid or realistic spray.

A later stage can investigate water motion, hydrostatic pressure, air displacement and trapped-air compression, with independently validated two-phase coupling before enabling simultaneous physical air and water forces.
