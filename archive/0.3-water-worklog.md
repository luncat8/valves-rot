## Slice 2 — water state without rendering (implemented)
- New `prototype/shared/water.js` (`ValveWater`), off by default via
  `S.setWater(sim, on)`; `sim.water` is null until enabled so the dry baseline
  is untouched. Wired into `simulation.step`, `reset`, the page script list and
  the render-smoke loader.
- Coarse world grid (default 6 px, a create parameter) sampled from the shared
  `airClearance` predicate — NOT the gas graph. Each cell = equal-area rectangle
  over its sampled vertical extent; water is a per-cell slice-area volume, the
  free surface is derived. Vertical faces join columns; horizontal faces record
  their deepest open point as a spill sill. A bounded Gauss–Seidel relaxation
  moves water high→low surface; a source never drains below its face sill, so
  perched pools and sill spill emerge instead of one global level.
- Per-cell state makes rotor split/merge conserve by construction. A closing
  wall displaces its water into adjacent void then routes via open faces only;
  any unreachable remainder is held as `trapped` (the rotor carries it) and
  returned when the passage reopens — no loss, no teleport through a shut seam.
- Counters (slice areas): `attempted/admitted/unadmitted/stored/overflowed/
  drained`, with `admitted = stored + overflowed + drained` and `attempted =
  admitted + unadmitted`. A metal mouth is blocked (unadmitted); an open mouth
  admits everything and a full system backs the excess out the top (overflow).
- Grain displacement is one-way here: submerged disc area reduces cell capacity
  and raises the pool waterline. Buoyancy force is Slice 3.
- Perf: per-valve-angle rebuild cache + a 30 Hz mask clock (relaxation still runs
  every step) + an air-cell list with dry-pair skip. Measured 0.48 ms/step static,
  1.42 ms/step with all valves turning (was 4.4 before the mask clock).

## Slice 3 — buoyancy + display (implemented, with a Slice-2 review pass first)

### Slice 2 review — three real defects, one cost regression
- **Valve replacement was invisible to the water mask.** The per-valve cache keyed
  only on `valve.angle`, and `placeValve`/`installValve` reuse the angle, so a
  wet chamber swapped for a solid plug (or a new chamber) left every cell's
  `air/area/width` describing the old rotor until that valve happened to turn.
  Added `sim.layoutVersion`, bumped in `installValve` (the one path that swaps a
  valve), and the cache now keys on `(angle, revision)`. Fixture: replace a wet
  hollow with a solid blank at the same angle and the cell must lose its air.
- **The per-cell surface is not a physics quantity for grains.** Buoyancy read
  `surface(cell)` for the grain's own cell. A cell holds one or two grains and
  shrinks by their displacement, so a lone float emptied its own cell and
  oscillated ~2 px at ~1 Hz. `publishLevels` now fills each vertical run from its
  floor (a cell takes its capacity first; the last cell that runs out of water
  carries the surface) and gives every cell of the run one level, with a short
  dry-gap skip upward. Same grain now settles to < 0.01 px and its floating
  fraction equals the density ratio exactly.
- **Rebuild cost moved from "every tick" to "per turned block", with a measured
  trade.** Per-valve cell blocks (contiguous index runs + a one-cell box) and
  per-cell/per-face stamps mean a turned rotor re-samples only its own ~150 cells
  and a face shared by two turned valves is sampled once, instead of the whole
  15,600-cell grid. Against the measured alternative (whole-grid sweep at 30 Hz)
  this is 1.21 vs 2.0 ms/step static and 2.4 vs 4.4 ms/step with every rotor
  turning; the mask clock also moved to 15 Hz because the water is quasi-static
  (66 ms of mask lag is invisible, and turning cost is paid per resample).
- Small cleanups while in there: full-grid `fill(0)`/`fill(NaN)` per step replaced
  by wet/displaced-cell lists (a 125 KB fill evicted the grain solver's hot
  data), dead `sim.volume` reference dropped, `segment` kernel shared with the
  solver's `submergedFraction`.

### Slice 3 — buoyancy, drag, exclusivity, fill
- `integrate` computes the submerged fraction once per grain from the published
  waterline and applies `g (1 - rho_w/rho_g f)`. With the solver's mass scale
  (`mass = (rho/1000) r^2`) the water density cancels, so the full-submersion
  acceleration reads directly off the palette: buoyant blue `-g`, neutral teal
  `0`, dense red `+0.5 g`. No per-grain mass needed beyond the existing arrays.
- Submerged drag uses the exponential step bound `1 - exp(-WATER_DRAG f h)`, which
  can only remove velocity, so water never injects energy. `WATER_DRAG = 6/s`
  gives ~47 px/s terminal sink and a still float inside 6 s.
- **Water and air are exclusive, both ways.** `setWater(on)` parks the pressure
  mode in `sim.airBeforeWater` and forces `off`; `setPressure` refuses to switch
  air on while water is enabled; `setWater(off)` restores the parked mode. The
  page disables the pressure radios while water is on and re-syncs on every mode
  change (Vent mode forces grid gas, so a water-on Vent page must not lie).
- **Display fill.** `sampleDisplay` builds a 3 px field from the solver's own
  passability plus the published levels per column, so the picture cannot claim
  water where the solver has none. `renderWater` maps depth below the waterline
  to alpha (0.58 -> 1.0 over 6 px) in a saturated blue; `renderWaterSurface`
  connects neighbouring column tops so the level reads as a line. Drawn after the
  scene and before the grains, so a submerged grain is seen through the sheet.
- Controls: Water on / Vent open / Rate (px²/s) / Drain water, plus the volume,
  wet-cell count and overflow/drained counters in the stats line. Help documents
  the density-to-floating-fraction mapping and the air-parking rule.
- `experiments/water-picture.cjs` writes `logs/water-state-0.3.png`: the seeded
  field mid-fill plus a 3x sealed pool with all six materials, so colour, alpha
  and the float/sink split can be judged without a browser.

### Known limit (measured, not fixed here)
A single floating grain of radius 3 px in a 6 px pool limit-cycles ~2 px: its
height exceeds one cell, capacity runs out before a level exists, and the level
it feels steps by a cell when it crosses an edge. Damping is bounded so the cycle
is slow; 2.3 px and 2.7 px floats in the same pool settle. Fix belongs with Slice
4 balance (pool geometry, cell size, or a level-interpolation term).
