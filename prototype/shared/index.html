Game goals v0.2

Each competitor is dealt 2 in (source) and 2 out (vent) mouths at independent random columns of the two bands, drawn in their color — four different columns per player, never an in mouth above one of that player's own out mouths. Press your own mouths to open or seal them; dealt mouths keep their in/out role, and rivals cannot press yours.

The random valve field stays in place. Each seat gets a prebuilt gas route through existing random valves to a hollow single · direction · random° collector and owns 2 in source mouths below plus 2 out vent mouths above, at four random columns; a mouth the route search could not join is only an owner ring, with no pipe into the field. Press your own mouths at any time, and on your turn turn one unlocked valve by 90° — the turn ends by itself and passes to the other seat. Route valves and collectors stay locked. Hold the collector's pressure inside its band for 4 s to score; a narrower band scores more. Other valves outside the protected routes still rotate on the normal 5% schedule. One or two seats only, because more players would share the pressure average.

Help

Playing

One geometry layer drives drawing and collision, and one fixed-step conservative solver drives the grains. Click or press-drag a valve — or focus the field and use arrow keys plus Enter/Space — to turn a rotor. A press-drag dials one 90° detent per quarter turn of the hand: its arc shows the pull, its bold notch the detent the rotor is committed to, and a short wobble never turns it. Pressing near the hub pulls horizontally instead, one detent per 52 px. Selection is explicit and sticky: a clicked valve stays selected while the cursor moves away (a faint ring marks the cell under the hand without committing), Shift-click or a still ~½ s press in Lab adds or removes cells from a multi-selection without turning anything, clicking empty space clears it, and the Heat panel carries Select all / Unselect buttons for the whole field. In Vent mode the field is turn based: the seat holding the turn turns exactly one unlocked valve by 90° (click, or Shift-click for the other way; the press-drag dial is off), the turn ends at once, and the Pass button or the hand-off clock gives it to the other seat. Route valves and collectors stay locked. Auto-rotate sets the share of valves that turn automatically on their schedule (90% default in Lab mode, 7% in Game mode, 5% selection in Vent mode, excluding protected routes).

In Game mode (G), sign an open quest to spawn one or two upright hollow single · direction ↑ N · 270° (ports N) goal bottles tinted in your player color (a pressure quest uses a four-way bottle). Build a route with the valves, press Pour for grains or connect a pressure source or sink to the target, and hold its count or local pressure to score. Pressure targets stay as advertised: no column is opened for you. Air from any open mouth can be routed, including a rival's, but only its owner can operate that mouth. Isolate competing branches when needed. Finished bottles become solid blank plugs and a new quest replaces the completed one.

The mouths in the pour and drain bands are constant-pressure boundary cells outside the 10×10 field: bottom rows hold high (1), top rows low (0). In Lab mode every column has a mouth: click one to seal or open it, Shift-click to swap its polarity. In Game mode the bands are dealt to the competitors instead — two in (source) mouths at the bottom and two out (vent) mouths at the top per player, at independent random columns and tinted in the owner's color — and only the owner can press them. A player's four mouths never share a column, and an in mouth never sits above that player's own out mouth; one column can still carry one player's source and another player's vent. Air only travels through open valve faces, so a shut port traps the pressure behind it.

In Vent mode, the random field and random valve types stay in place. Setup connects each seat's two random source mouths and two random vent mouths to a hollow single · direction · random° collector by routing through existing valves; it does not replace the route with easy straight pipes. Both seats keep their own mouths at all times, and valve play takes turns: the seat on the clock turns one unlocked valve by 90°, which ends its turn, and the other seat follows after a one second hand-off. Route valves and collectors stay aligned, other selected valves still rotate on the automatic schedule, and a turn left unspent passes after ten seconds. Hold the collector's pressure inside its band for 4 s to score; the next band moves a step. The gauge shows live pressure, the arc shows hold progress and the side readout names the band. One or two seats only, because players share the field's pressure average. Seats can be added or removed at any time, and each has its own bot checkbox.

Controls and air

Turn speed controls valves, not gravity. Step advances one step while paused. Switching the pressure mode does not reset grains or valve targets.

Grid gas solves a conservative diffusion of relative pressure through the valve pipes. Each rotor's passages are divided into cells in the rotor's own turning coordinates, while the short casing pipes stay still; a turn refreshes the seam links on each simulation tick. Open mouths are fixed at 1 or 0; air spreads through links that a small clearance disc can actually cross, so metal never leaks and a part-open port throttles the flow instead of snapping shut. Turning a valve carries the air inside its rotor with it, and even isolated connected pipes exchange pressure. With every mouth sealed, each disconnected pocket keeps its air while differences inside it gradually even out; the field is not instantly repainted. A packed pile raises resistance, and a turning rotor swirls air in its own disc and just outside open ports. Air particles shows a cyan kinetic population that circulates from bottom sources to top vents and knocks grains about. Off leaves the grains untouched. Air force scales the push from 0× (off) to 2×: with Grid gas selected, 1× balances gravity for a standard grain in the vertical vent test, while 2× gives lighter grains net lift.

Water is a separate, quasi-static mode: the open source vents pour slice area at their own rates into the pockets under their columns, pools fill up to the lowest open face they can spill over, and a grain floats when the water it displaces balances its own mass. Colour is density, so buoyant blue floats with half its disc under the line, neutral teal hangs at any depth, green and pink sink slowly, and red and amber sink fast. Submerged drag is bounded and only removes speed. Air pressure mode is parked while water is on (the backends are exclusive until a gas/water interface exists) and comes back when water is switched off. Water remains visible after the vent is closed; Drain water empties every pool and counts it as drained. Water has no velocity, so it separates a mixed bed in place rather than carrying it: in a pool two or three chambers deep the light materials gather at the surface and the dense ones settle on the floor, and the same bed dropped dry stays mixed at the bottom. A float can be lifted but not pushed sideways, so a side pocket at the surface level never takes one: to keep the light layer, build a one-chamber-wide shaft with a gate valve, fill to just above the gate, pour the mixture down the shaft (the dense grains sink, the floats stay up), turn the gate a quarter turn shut under the layer, then Drain water. Keep the final rise slower than the lightest float or the surface leaves the layer behind.

Water colours are three conserved channels, R, G and B, not a tint of one volume: every move of liquid carries its channels in proportion, so a drain, an overflow, a sill spill or a rotor closing a wet cell cannot lose or invent a colour. The R/G/B boxes split the same total Rate equally between the selected colours, so unticking one does not pour less water, it pours a different mix; the last box cannot be unticked. The fill shows each cell's channels normalized so the strongest one is full: equal shares read white, R+G reads yellow and a single channel reads its own hue, which means a strong and a weak solution look alike. The sheet is painted at a fixed 55% shade so an equal mix still reads darker than the pale porcelain instead of vanishing into it. Pigment view instead paints one grey level, L = clamp((765 − (R + G + B)) / 2, 0, 255), from that same normalized triple: white reads black, (80,80,80) reads full light, (255,255,0) reads about half, and a pure primary reads light. Both are views only; neither changes the water. The Mix R/G/B boxes decide which colours form one liquid: every checked colour joins a single mixed phase at 1,000 kg/m³, and each unchecked colour becomes its own phase — R at 2,000, G at 1,000, B at 500 kg/m³, the grain palette's own densities (green liquid matches the neutral teal grain, not the 800 kg/m³ green grain). Unchecked colours settle into layers, red below green below blue, over a few seconds; ticking a box only regroups the channels and can neither move nor lose liquid. Each layer buoys grains by its own density, so a blue grain hangs neutral in blue liquid while a teal one sinks through it. The status line reports each channel's share of the stored liquid.

Water vents are the row of source tabs in the strip above the top air mouths, one per column. Click a tab to make it the current vent — the one the global water controls (Vent open, Column, Rate, R/G/B) edit. Slide left/right on a tab to sweep its pour colour around the RGB wheel (the tab shows the spectrum and a cursor while you drag); slide up/down to set its rate between 0 and the global Rate ceiling; double-click for a colour picker — a picked colour maps to proportional channel shares, pure black is refused because it carries no ink ratio, and brightness normalizes away as it always does in the fill; long-click (hold ~½ s without moving) to open or shut the vent, which a sealed cross and a dimmed tab mark. The Column slider is a brush: moving it stamps the current vent's settings onto every vent it passes, so setting red and sweeping the column from 1 to 10 turns the whole row red — useful to set up many vents at once. Clicking a tab selects without stamping. Every open vent pours its own rate and colour into its column's mouth cell; the R/G/B boxes mirror the current vent's channels and snap it to equal shares, and the pour boxes' last-colour rule keeps at least one channel on.

Heat adds an illustrative thermal field on the water grid: every cell holds one total enthalpy number covering its metal, its liquid and the grains sitting in it, and temperature is derived from that number and the cell's composition — never stored beside it, so a phase-change plateau is a real buffer rather than a display trick. Enabling Heat enables Water with every vent shut; turning Water off parks Heat while each valve keeps its power, and Reset empties the field but keeps the powers too. Slide Power to heat (+, red arrow badge) or cool (−, blue badge) — Power edits every selected valve at once: a click selects one valve and it stays selected while the cursor moves, Shift-click or a still ~½ s press in Lab adds or removes cells without turning anything (a faint ring marks the hovered cell, a light blue ring each extra selected cell, and the strong ring the anchor the readout names), clicking empty space clears the selection, and the Select all / Unselect buttons set or clear the whole field. The badge sits in the case corner and a ring marks every valve the panel will edit. Ambient sets the room temperature the field exchanges with through its outer ring. Temperature view paints the whole machine — housings and passages alike — on a fixed 0–240 °C scale and dims the liquid sheet so colour and temperature read together; the °C legend takes the pressure legend's slot, which is free because the air backends are parked while water is on. The scales are illustrative and shared with the water reference: 1 mm/px, 10 mm slice depth, 1 mg per mass unit.

Melting and freezing are energy-exact. A grain melts only when its cell holds, beyond its melt point, the whole latent heat the grain's mass needs plus the capacity swap — until then the cell pins exactly at the melt point and the excess waits as pending latent energy. Materials melt into liquid channels by mass: red into R, green into G, blue into B, amber into half R half G, teal into half G half B, pink into half R half B — and a material melts at the highest plateau among its channels, so what it becomes is stable. The channel plateaus default to R 120, G 100, B 80 °C, above the 20 °C room: at room temperature every melt product wants to freeze again. Freezing converts channels back into primary grains — R to red, G to green, B to blue — highest plateau first, so a cooling mixture sheds its red before its green; amber, teal and pink never re-form as alloys. A melt too small for one minimum-size grain stays liquid, pinned at its plateau, and a placement with nowhere to fit a grain defers without consuming mass or energy. Every liquid move carries its enthalpy — supply enters at ambient with its latent, overflow and drain carry the donor's, a grain crossing cells moves its sensible share once, and the status line's ledger holds: stored = opening + sources + room + material, with melting and freezing internal and cancelling. No convection, radiation, evaporation, alloy synthesis, heat carried by rotating metal or independent grain temperatures are modelled, and the pressure display remains a relative measurement, not a thermodynamic one.

Lubricant reduces tangential grain-to-grain and grain-to-wall friction (0–2; default 1). Contact forces are solved for touching grains before overlap develops, so a loaded bend can transmit gravity without shaking. Lubricant stress is an optional deterministic micro-shake (0–0.5× the old kick, default off) for rare stubborn jams. A level pipe still needs pressure or a lower outlet to flow.

Grid colors: relative pressure

The grid stores a normalized relative pressure, not a measured thermodynamic one. The legend maps 0–1 to 0–the reference span in gauge-equivalent pascals; changing the span only relabels the ramp and does not change forces. With no open mouth the ramp still describes the trapped air; the legend names the missing source or vent when only one polarity is open.

The default span is measured from normal particle-wall impulse in a sealed reference chamber: 100 markers at 180 px/s, radius 15 px, over 20 simulation seconds. Its illustrative scales are 1 mm/px, 1 simulation second = 1 s, 1 mg per mass unit (3 mg per marker), and 10 mm depth. It is not an atmospheric pressure or a validation of pressure everywhere in the machine. Enter a known source-to-vent gauge difference to use another reference.

Grain materials

Color is material, not just a label: red 2,000 · amber 1,400 · teal 1,000 · pink 1,200 · green 800 · blue 500 kg/m³ (water is 1,000). Mass follows density × disc size, so a large light grain can outweigh a small dense one. Air pushes light grains harder than dense ones of the same size.

Floating is decided by density, not by size: a completely submerged grain accelerates at g (1 - 1,000/density), so blue rises fastest, teal is weightless, and red sinks at half gravity. A floating grain settles where its submerged disc fraction equals density / 1,000: blue 50%, green 80%, teal 100%; anything denser than water sinks to the floor.

Other info

The line under the field reports fps, the seed, active / injected / drained grains, plus solver diagnostics: purged (removed by Drain or by replacing a valve), recovered and pushes (grains nudged out of a turning wall). In grid mode it also shows mean pressure and flow.

Valve type names (solid / hollow × straight, elbow, tee, cross, single, blank) are listed in the Place valve drawer, where each name can be copied to the clipboard as text.
