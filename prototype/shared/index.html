Help

Playing

One geometry layer drives drawing and collision, and one fixed-step conservative solver drives the grains. Click or press-drag a valve — or focus the field and use arrow keys plus Enter/Space — to turn a rotor. Auto-rotate sets the share of valves that turn automatically on their schedule (90% default in Lab mode, 20% in Game mode).

In Game mode (G), sign an open quest to spawn one or two upright hollow single · direction ↑ N · 270° (ports N) goal bottles tinted in your player color (a pressure quest uses a four-way bottle and opens a vertical air shaft in its column). Build a route with the valves, press Pour for more grains or toggle air mouths, and hold the target count or pressure to score. A pressure target is fitted to what its cell can reach when you sign: open the band that feeds the required polarity, then shut the opposing band mouth by mouth, nearest first, and leave the farthest one open as the field's reference. Finished goal bottles turn into solid blank plugs and a new random quest replaces the completed one.

The mouths in the pour and drain bands are constant-pressure boundary cells outside the 10×10 field: bottom rows hold high (1), top rows low (0). Click a mouth to seal or open it, Shift-click to swap its polarity. Air only travels through open valve faces, so a shut port traps the pressure behind it.

Controls and air

Turn speed controls valves, not gravity. Step advances one step while paused. Switching the pressure mode does not reset grains or valve targets.

Grid gas solves a conservative diffusion of relative pressure on a 6 px grid. Open mouths are fixed at 1 or 0; air spreads from them through open faces only, so turning a valve moves the front and a shut rim leaves the pocket behind at its old pressure. Metal never leaks. Seal every mouth and the machine simply holds the air it has — no pressure through-flow, no flat repaint — while a packed pile raises the resistance of its cells, and a turning rotor keeps swirling the air in its own disc. Air particles shows a cyan kinetic population that circulates from bottom sources to top vents and knocks grains about. Off leaves the grains untouched. Air force scales the push on grains from 0 (they fall as if pressure were off) to 2 (updrafts can hold a grain).

Grid colors: relative pressure

The grid stores a normalized relative pressure, not a measured thermodynamic one. The legend maps 0–1 to 0–the reference span in gauge-equivalent pascals; changing the span only relabels the ramp and does not change forces. With no open mouth the ramp still describes the trapped air; the legend names the missing source or vent when only one polarity is open.

The default span is measured from normal particle-wall impulse in a sealed reference chamber: 100 markers at 180 px/s, radius 15 px, over 20 simulation seconds. Its illustrative scales are 1 mm/px, 1 simulation second = 1 s, 1 mg per mass unit (3 mg per marker), and 10 mm depth. It is not an atmospheric pressure or a validation of pressure everywhere in the machine. Enter a known source-to-vent gauge difference to use another reference.

Other info

The line under the field reports fps, the seed, active / injected / drained grains, plus solver diagnostics: purged (removed by Drain or by replacing a valve), recovered and pushes (grains nudged out of a turning wall). In grid mode it also shows mean pressure and flow.

Valve type names (solid / hollow × straight, elbow, tee, cross, single, blank) are listed in the Place valve drawer, where each name can be copied to the clipboard as text.
